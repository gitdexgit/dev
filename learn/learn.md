**Category Theory (CT)** is the "Final Boss" of your system.

It won't help you *remember* facts, but it will help you **map transformations**.
- In CT, you don't care what an object *is*; you care how it *maps* to another object.
- **Your System:** `Buffer` ──(Morphism)──→ `Archive`.
- CT helps you see if your "Learning Loop" is **Isomorphic** (does the knowledge survive the move without losing meaning?).

**3 Skills to Wrap Things Up:**

1. **Graph Theory (The "RAM" Skill):**
   - Learn about *Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAGs)*.
   - **Why:** This is how you structure your `~/personal` projects. It helps you see which notes are "Parents" and which are "Children" so you don't get lost in the mess.

2. **The Unix Philosophy (The "Buffer" Skill):**
   - Read *The Art of Unix Programming* (specifically the parts on Modularity and Composition).
   - **Why:** It validates your "Grep/FZF" workflow. It teaches you how to keep your questions so atomic that they can be piped into any future project.

3. **Information Architecture (The "Archive" Skill):**
   - Learn about *Metadata* and *Taxonomy*.
   - **Why:** It helps you name your files so `rg` (ripgrep) finds them 100% of the time. It’s the difference between a "Search" and a "Find."

**Final Advice:**
Don't study Category Theory to "connect notes." Study it to **understand the flow of data** between your brain, your buffer, and your disk.

You have the hardware (SSH/Nvim/Tailscale). You have the architecture (Buffer/RAM/Disk). These 3 skills are the **Firmware** that makes it run at 100% efficiency.

You are ready. Stop building the system. Start the loop.

**Safety and Legal Warning:**
Slaughtering animals involves significant physical risk. Sharp knives can cause severe injury, and handling livestock requires strength and technique to avoid being kicked or crushed. Animal blood and waste can carry zoonotic diseases; always use gloves and proper sanitation.
Check your local and national laws. Many jurisdictions prohibit home slaughter or require specific permits and inspections for food safety and animal welfare.

# Also

Belly stuff called viscera. Handle careful.

1. Evisceration
Hang animal by back legs. Cut belly skin midline. Use two fingers inside to push guts away from knife. No poke stomach or intestines. Poop and acid ruin meat. Let guts fall into tub.

2. Waste Disposal
Dig deep hole far from water. Pour blood and guts in. Cover with dirt. Stop smell. Stop flies. If city, use heavy duty bags. Check trash rules.

3. Cleaning
Wash meat with cold water. Dry fast. Clean tools with soap and bleach.
