It is a very common thing to mishear, especially because the speech is about brainwashing and social control—but the correct term is **Human Condition**, not "Human Conditioning."

Here is why the distinction matters and why the writer chose "Condition":

### 1. The Meaning of "Human Condition"

As mentioned before, the **human condition** refers to the essence of being human—our soul, our struggles, our deep emotions, and our mortality.

- **The Metaphor:** In the speech, Mr. Robot says we tossed the "remnants" (the leftover pieces) of our humanity into a dumpster. You toss a **thing** into a dumpster. Our "condition" (our humanity) is the thing we threw away.


### 2. What would "Human Conditioning" mean?

**Human conditioning** is a psychological term. It refers to the process of training or "programming" people to act a certain way (like how you condition a dog to sit).

- **Why it's not the word used:** The rest of the speech actually describes the **process** of conditioning (the "brainwashing seminars" and "advertising").

- **The Logic:** You wouldn't throw the conditioning into the dumpster. The conditioning is the **trash collector**—it is the force that is pushing us to throw away our "human condition."


### 3. Why it sounds like "Conditioning"

There are two reasons you might hear "conditioning":

1. **Phonetics:** When Christian Slater (the actor) says "Condition," the "n" sound at the end can blend into the next word "We" or "While," making it sound like a trailing syllable.

2. **Thematic Context:** Because he is talking about "brainwashing" and "hypnotizing" us, your brain naturally looks for words like "conditioning" because they fit the topic of mental control.

This part of the speech is a direct attack on how **consumerism and the digital economy** have taken over our lives. It’s Mr. Robot’s way of saying that even our most private spaces and our sense of value are no longer "real."

Here is a breakdown of what those specific lines mean:

### 1. "We live in branded houses, trademarked by corporations..."
*   **The Literal Meaning:** Think about how much of your home is covered in logos. Your TV is a Sony, your fridge is a Samsung, your clothes are Nike, your food is Nestlé.
*   **The Metaphor:** He is saying that we don't just *buy* things; we have let corporations own our **identity**. We no longer live in a "home" (a place of real human connection); we live in a "brand." Every part of our life is sponsored, tracked, and owned by a company. We have become walking advertisements for the people who sell us things.

### 2. "...built on bipolar numbers jumping up and down on digital displays"
This is a very specific critique of the **Stock Market and the Financial System.**
*   **"Bipolar numbers":** He uses the word "bipolar" to describe the extreme, irrational mood swings of the economy. One day the market is at an "all-time high" (mania), and the next day it crashes (depression).
*   **"Digital displays":** He is pointing out that our wealth isn't real anymore. It isn't gold or land or anything you can touch. It’s just pixels on a screen—numbers moving up and down in a computer bank.
*   **The Point:** He is saying that the entire foundation of our society is "fake." We base our lives, our jobs, and our survival on these "moody" digital numbers that have no actual physical reality.
